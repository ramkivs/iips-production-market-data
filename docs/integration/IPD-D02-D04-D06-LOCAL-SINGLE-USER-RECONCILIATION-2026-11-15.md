## D04 — Relationship multiplicity and active uniqueness

**1. Existing approved requirements.** D01 approves the existing S1 membership as relationship subject with checks of its binding, principal, tenant, S1 owner and ACTIVE states. D02 prohibits a second D02 principal, binding or membership and confirms that a relationship record ID is distinct from an identity ID. D03 approves tenant-scoped composite membership and owner references, matching tenant values and restrictive deletes. The approved architecture directs a separate link from the existing local identity to `owner-4dc756b3`; it does not decide the detailed indexes or provision a relationship. Existing migration 003 already permits at most one ACTIVE local binding and membership per database.

**2. Actual local risk.** More than one ACTIVE relationship for the configured S1 membership could yield ambiguous owner resolution. An active relation for a different owner or tenant could misroute access. A relationship can also remain marked ACTIVE after its membership becomes inactive, leaving a conflicting stale row even though migration 003 permits only one ACTIVE membership. This is a possible integrity failure, not a claim that such rows exist. The four portfolios are distinct data objects under one overall owner; they do not increase relationship cardinality.

**3. Minimum sufficient rule.** Enforce at most one ACTIVE relationship row for `(membership, relationship type)` and at most one for `(tenant, overall owner, relationship type)`. Require the resolver to find exactly one attached, same-tenant, ACTIVE relationship to the approved owner, with the D01 S1 checks and D03 ACTIVE checks, otherwise refuse without data or effect. The first constraint plus exact S1 resolution prevents ambiguity in access; the second is a small integrity guard against a stale ACTIVE row attached to an inactive membership coexisting with another ACTIVE link. Migration 003’s single-active-membership constraint does not itself change relationship status. The guard is not evidence of another identity, device or tenant, and multiple historical REVOKED relationship rows may remain. Retaining both proposed partial unique indexes, or an equivalent non-bypassable conflict prevention, avoids conflicting ACTIVE rows; their exact DDL remains pending.

**4. Remove or defer.** Do not add per-portfolio owner relationships, principal/device cardinality, tenant administration, or support for multiple owners. Do not relax the singleton constraints implicitly. A future multi-principal/device deployment would need a new design and authority decision.

**5. Dependencies.** D01 subject and exact S1 checks; D02 no-new-identity rule; D03 tenant-scoped keys and ACTIVE checks; D05 lifecycle; D08 resolver behavior. The exact DDL and SQLite index/FK support remain part of pending detailed-schema validation.

**6. Recommended exact disposition wording (proposed, not approved):**

> **D04 — APPROVE (proposed):** For the current personal, local, single-user scope, enforce at most one ACTIVE `OVERALL_PERSONAL_PORTFOLIO_OWNER` relationship per existing S1 membership and at most one ACTIVE relationship per tenant and overall portfolio owner for that relationship type. Resolve only the single attached, same-tenant ACTIVE relationship to the approved D02 owner after the approved S1 and owner-state checks; missing, duplicate, inactive, mismatched or conflicting state fails closed. Retain REVOKED history. One relationship supplies the common owner context for the four separately identified portfolios. Do not add multi-principal, multi-device, per-portfolio relationship or multi-tenant administration semantics. A scope expansion requires a new design and authority decision.

**7. Unresolved.** D04 is formally pending because the approval record explicitly approves only D01–D03. The PA must explicitly accept, amend, or defer/reject the proposed reciprocal guard; exact S1 resolution alone prevents access ambiguity, while the guard additionally rejects conflicting stored ACTIVE rows. No operational database was examined to establish whether stale rows exist. Approval of the architectural direction does not approve these indexes or DDL. Disposable SQLite validation and full schema approval remain outstanding.

## D05 — Owner registry lifecycle and revocation

**1. Existing approved requirements.** D03 approves an owner-registry key and tenant-scoped reference, with restrictive delete; FKs alone do not prove active authorization, so resolver status checks are required. D01 requires ACTIVE checks for the S1 binding and membership. The operating model requires preserving identity, ownership, audit history and recoverability. It does not erase the specific D02/D05 provisioning/migration restriction; D22 remains unresolved.

**2. Actual local risk.** If the S1 membership, owner-registry entry, or relationship becomes inactive, continued resolution could use an invalid mapping. A stale ACTIVE relationship must not authorize access or be silently selected as fallback. There is one expected local owner, not a population of administrators or owners to manage.

**3. Minimum sufficient rule.** Keep owner-registry state and validate it as ACTIVE alongside the S1 binding, membership and relationship on every D02 resolution. A relationship is ACTIVE with no revocation time or REVOKED with a revocation time; preserve the row and never silently reactivate it. For a separately authorized revocation, change its state and append the audit event in the same transaction; subsequent resolution refuses. If the owner entry is revoked, fail closed and, when that transition is separately authorized, revoke all linked ACTIVE relationships and append the owner and relationship events in the same transaction. A later relationship re-establishment requires an ACTIVE owner entry, a new relationship record and applicable explicit authority. The owner entry’s own recovery/terminality is not decided here.

**4. Remove or defer.** No owner-administrator UI, delegated revoker, multi-actor workflow, approval tier, reason-code taxonomy, or dynamic owner registry lifecycle is justified for one fixed local owner. Do not infer authority to create/revoke records from this design recommendation. The general owner-directed operating model does not resolve the D22 restriction.

**5. Dependencies.** D01 and D03 ACTIVE-state requirements; D04 uniqueness; D06 event history; D08 fresh fail-closed resolution; existing transaction and audit guarantees. D22’s specific operational provisioning/migration boundary remains independent and unresolved.

**6. Recommended exact disposition wording (proposed, not approved):**

> **D05 — AMEND (proposed; owner-entry recovery unresolved):** Retain the single approved tenant-scoped owner-registry key and require ACTIVE S1 binding, membership, owner-registry entry and ownership relationship for every D02 resolution. Preserve each relationship as ACTIVE with no revocation time or terminally REVOKED with a revocation time; an authorized revocation updates status/time and appends an event atomically. If an owner entry is separately authorized to be revoked, fail closed and atomically revoke all linked ACTIVE relationships and append corresponding owner and relationship events. Do not silently reactivate a revoked relationship or owner entry. A new relationship requires an ACTIVE owner entry, a new relationship record and applicable explicit authority. Whether and how the same fixed owner entry can be restored after revocation remains unresolved; do not invent a replacement owner or tenant. No administrator role, delegated revocation workflow, or multi-actor lifecycle service is required for the current scope.

**7. Unresolved.** The package asks for terminal owner revocation, while the local review requires ACTIVE checks for the fixed owner but finds no evidence for a registry-admin lifecycle. The proposed amendment retains fail-closed behavior and atomic consistency for any authorized revocation without requiring an administrative system. It does not decide whether a mistakenly revoked owner entry is permanently terminal or can be restored: the owner key is fixed, the evidence defines no correction path, and inventing a replacement owner would violate the approved mapping. That owner-entry question and the overall D05 disposition require an explicit PA decision. No operational authority follows; D22 must be resolved separately before restricted provisioning or migration.

## D06 — Relationship identifiers, authority reference and audit

**1. Existing approved requirements.** D02 approves a separate opaque relationship record ID, not another identity ID. D03 requires tenant-matching references. The existing local persistence has an append-only SHA-256 hash-chained `audit_events` sequence with event/subject, JSON detail, UTC timestamp, previous checksum and entry checksum. The operating model requires proportionate traceability and preservation. The architecture proposal expressly leaves the relationship ID unassigned; no ID or row is created by this report.

**2. Actual local risk.** A relationship needs stable distinct identity across its ACTIVE and REVOKED history, and any transition needs enough local evidence to explain what record changed and recover/verify state. Reusing the S1 membership ID as the relationship ID would conflate identity and ownership and would not distinguish a later relationship lifecycle. Credentials, broker tokens, or fabricated actor facts must never enter the record.

**3. Minimum sufficient rule.** Require a stable, non-empty, unique, non-semantic relationship record key distinct from S1 identity values, with duplicate collision aborting the transaction. The exact encoding is a detailed-schema choice; UUIDv4 is acceptable if selected, but is not itself a security boundary or credential. Use an actual explicit PA disposition record identifier as the non-empty authority reference; it records design provenance and is not operational authorization. Reuse the existing audit chain. For each separately authorized relationship creation/revocation, append within the same transaction an event recording action, relationship key, existing S1 membership and binding, tenant and owner, prior/new state, decision reference and UTC time. If an applicable operational authorization is separately recorded, retain its provenance too; do not invent a reference. Likewise audit any authorized owner-state transition atomically. Do not add an actor identity or reason code without a supported source and demonstrated need. Keep the operating model’s verified recoverable backup and usable restore safeguards for destructive or difficult-to-reverse work: a local hash chain can detect inconsistent links but is neither an independent backup nor proof against complete rewrite or truncation.

**4. Remove or defer.** No separate audit table or external authority service/catalogue; no content-digest requirement for the authority reference; no actor directory, signatures, actor hierarchy, or mandatory reason taxonomy. Preserve the existing local hash chain and transaction semantics. UUIDv4 can be selected for implementation consistency but should not be elevated to an authentication secret, anti-corruption guarantee, or separate PA requirement. D06 also proposes source-record/account IDs: do not mandate new source-account or registry IDs for this ownership link; their need and format depend on pending D10–D13. Preserve existing portfolio and source/provenance identifiers and evidence.

**5. Dependencies.** D02 distinct opaque record identity; D03 tenant-scoped references; D04 uniqueness; D05 lifecycle; D10–D13 for any future source-record/account identity; existing migration 002 audit sequence and writer; D09 transaction consistency; detailed schema’s SQLite constraints and migration validation. D22 remains independent of a design-provenance reference.

**6. Recommended exact disposition wording (proposed, not approved):**

> **D06 — AMEND (proposed):** Each ownership relationship has a stable, non-empty, unique, non-semantic opaque record identifier distinct from all S1 identity values; duplicate identifier insertion aborts the transaction. The local representation may be UUIDv4 or another suitable unique surrogate and is not a credential. `authority_record_ref` identifies an actual explicit PA disposition record; this provenance does not grant implementation or operational authority. Reuse the existing append-only local hash-chained audit sequence. A separately authorized relationship creation or revocation appends, in the same transaction, an event with action, relationship ID, existing membership and binding, matching tenant and owner, prior/new relationship state, decision reference and UTC time; record applicable operational authorization provenance when separately established. Audit any authorized owner-state transition in the same transaction. Preserve verified backup/restore safeguards; the local chain is not an independent backup or proof of tamper resistance. Do not require an actor directory or reason-code taxonomy without evidence of a supported source. Store no credentials or account tokens. Defer source-record/account identifier requirements to the related D10–D13 decisions.

**7. Unresolved.** The package’s proposed UUIDv4, digest-bearing authority reference, actor reference and reason code are not evidenced as local needs; the existing audit table’s available fields and hash-chain behavior are evidenced, but do not establish the source of an actor or reason. The exact identifier encoding, existing PA disposition to cite once recorded, event-detail representation and any separately established operation-authority provenance require further decision and detailed-schema review. The need for new source-record/account IDs depends on pending D10–D13. SQLite behavior remains unvalidated in a disposable database, and no live records or backup were inspected. D06 remains pending until the PA explicitly approves/amends the recommendation; no ID is generated here.
