## D07 — Private persistence boundary and referential integrity

**1. Existing approved requirements and exact sources.** [D01–D03’s approval record](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-APPROVAL-RECORD-2026-11-16.md#recorded-approvals) and [package D03](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-DECISION-PACKAGE-2026-11-16.md#d03--relationship-and-owner-tenantfk-requirements--approved) approve composite membership/tenant and owner/tenant relationship FKs, matching tenant values, parent unique keys and restrictive deletes; they also require ACTIVE-state checks because FKs do not prove authorization. The [architecture direction](IPD-D02-OWNERSHIP-ARCHITECTURE-DECISION-2026-11-15.md#1-approved-architectural-direction) preserves S1 and a distinct D02 relationship. The existing [local persistence contract §§1, 7.2–7.4](../LOCAL-PERSONAL-USE-PERSISTENCE.md#72-the-authorization-path) retains server-owned write authorization, scope separation and default-deny. [Package D07](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-DECISION-PACKAGE-2026-11-16.md#d07--portfolio-to-owner-registry-fk-versus-complete-access-facade) and [review D07](IPD-D02-LOCAL-SINGLE-USER-SCOPE-APPLICABILITY-REVIEW-2026-11-15.md#d07--simplify-owner-access-boundary-not-enterprise-capability-security) are unapproved alternatives and applicability advice, not additional approvals.

**2. Current single-user applicability.** Wrong owner selection is a data-integrity risk even without another user. Repository [migration 001](../../src/persistence/migrations/001_initial_schema.ts) keys portfolios by `(canonical_owner_id, portfolio_id)`, stores a tenant, and has owner/portfolio and revision child FKs; it has no portfolio-to-owner-registry FK. [The store](../../src/persistence/portfolio_store.ts) exposes owner-taking methods, and [the dispatcher](../../src/ipc/dispatch.ts) constructs a store internally. The [public UI package](../../src/ui/portfolio_ui_package.ts) excludes persistence exports, but that alone does not prove all server-side paths cannot bypass a future D02 boundary. These are source observations, not proof of an exploitable route or of any live row.

**3. Minimum sufficient design.**

- **Application ownership boundary:** A private server-side D02 entrypoint derives its owner/tenant from the freshly validated relationship in the same transaction or read snapshot as the operation. Route every D02 list/get/history/contribution, create/save/reset, restore/delete, manual and broker-import path through it; never trust an IPC-supplied owner, relationship or reusable stale context. A target portfolio ID may be requested, but must be checked against the resolved scope and applicable status/revision. Keep existing write guards and identity qualification. Direct P1 continues on its existing S1 path. A TypeScript type alone is not proof of an unforgeable boundary; demonstrate that production callers cannot reach a raw owner-taking alternative or reuse a context outside its transaction.
- **Database integrity:** Retain D03’s approved tenant-scoped composite relationship FKs, matching-tenant check, required parent uniqueness and `RESTRICT` deletes; verify FK enforcement on every applicable SQLite connection. Preserve the current portfolio/child keys and FKs. The current normal connection enables foreign keys, but actual runtime and proposed DDL still need validation. A facade does **not** replace these D03 database requirements.
- **Separate portfolio-to-registry question:** The additional FK from `portfolios` to the proposed owner registry is **not** one of D03’s approved relationship FKs. A complete boundary may be sufficient for controlled D02 access without a table rebuild, but without that additional FK the database alone does not prevent an orphan or wrong-owner portfolio row inserted through an uncontrolled path. An FK can reduce that structural risk; it cannot prove ACTIVE state, prevent an unauthorized read, or distinguish a valid-but-wrong owner. If the boundary cannot be proven complete, stop and repair the access path; evaluate an additional compatible portfolio FK as a separate integrity measure, not as a substitute for authorization.

**4. Complexity to simplify or defer.** Do not require a new service, external policy engine, tenant directory, administrator workflow, transaction-nonce capability registry, or portfolio-table rebuild solely for the current local scope. Stronger machinery or an extra FK may be justified by a demonstrated bypass or separate data-integrity requirement, but is not assumed. Preserve the existing guards rather than removing them.

**5. Dependencies.** Approved D01–D03 define the identity and D03 FK minimum. D04–D06’s proposed uniqueness, lifecycle and audit rules remain **PENDING**. D08 supplies resolution; D09 binds it to the operation; D10–D13 affect broker/manual paths and source checks; D18 covers any later schema mechanics. D21/D22 and the current implementation/operational restrictions remain independent.

**6. Exact recommended decision wording (proposed, not approved):**

> **D07 — AMEND (proposed):** For the local single-user application, require one private, demonstrably complete server-side D02 access boundary for every portfolio read and write. Resolve and use ownership only from the validated S1-to-D02 relationship within the operation’s transaction or read snapshot; reject caller-selected owner or relationship authority and prevent D02 callers outside that boundary from directly invoking owner-taking store methods. Preserve existing write guards, Direct P1 and the approved D03 composite tenant-scoped relationship FKs, matching tenant values, parent keys and restrictive deletes. Do not require an additional portfolio-to-registry FK, transaction-nonce capability system or new service unless a concrete integrity need warrants it. If complete boundary coverage cannot be demonstrated, D02 access remains blocked until the bypass is closed. A separately evaluated portfolio FK can prevent some orphan rows but cannot replace owner/status authorization or secure reads.

**7. Outstanding evidence and consequences.** The complete D02 production call graph, bypass prevention and tenant-scoped read/write tests are not established; an internal `OwnerRef` method or a merely typed context is not sufficient evidence. The exact proposed D03 DDL, FK enablement on relevant connections and any need for a portfolio-to-registry FK require later disposable validation and design review; no operational database was inspected. Package D07 recommends the portfolio FK fallback if its capability facade cannot be proven. This proposed amendment instead requires a complete access boundary in either case because an FK alone cannot secure reads or active-owner selection. The Program Authority must explicitly disposition that difference; until then D07 remains pending and no D02 rollout is authorized.

## D08 — D02 resolver and authorization contract

**1. Existing approved requirements and exact sources.** [Package D01](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-DECISION-PACKAGE-2026-11-16.md#d01--relationship-subject-and-exact-identity-fields--approved), [D02](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-DECISION-PACKAGE-2026-11-16.md#d02--no-new-d02-principal-binding-or-membership-identity-value-contract--approved), [D03](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-DECISION-PACKAGE-2026-11-16.md#d03--relationship-and-owner-tenantfk-requirements--approved), and their [approval record](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-APPROVAL-RECORD-2026-11-16.md#recorded-approvals) establish the existing membership subject, exact S1 tuple checks, no new D02 identity, tenant matching and owner/binding/membership ACTIVE checks. [Product ownership](IPD-D02-OVERALL-PERSONAL-PORTFOLIO-OWNERSHIP-DETERMINATION-2026-11-15.md#1-approved-product-ownership-decision) preserves distinct portfolio/source identities and Direct P1. [Package D08](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-DECISION-PACKAGE-2026-11-16.md#d08--d02-resolver-and-authorization-contract) and [review D08](IPD-D02-LOCAL-SINGLE-USER-SCOPE-APPLICABILITY-REVIEW-2026-11-15.md#d08--essential-relationship-aware-owner-resolution) propose further resolver details; those details are not yet approved.

**2. Current single-user applicability.** The existing [binding resolver](../../src/persistence/binding.ts) requires the membership owner itself to equal `owner-4dc756b3` for holdings. The approved S1 membership owner is instead `ipd-owner-ramki-personal`, so that current predicate refuses the established S1 tuple; it is not evidence of a working relationship resolver. The [architecture record](IPD-D02-OWNERSHIP-ARCHITECTURE-DECISION-2026-11-15.md#4-current-behavior-remains-fail-closed) keeps that existing fail-closed behavior until separately approved and implemented. No live binding or relationship was inspected.

**3. Minimum sufficient design.** In the D09 transaction/snapshot, resolve the single ACTIVE S1 binding and membership; verify their attachment and every exact approved S1 value listed above. Consider all ACTIVE overall-owner relationships for that membership/type before choosing: require exactly one, then verify its membership, matching tenant, relationship state, active owner-registry entry and expected D02 owner. Do not filter away a competing active relationship by first selecting only the desired owner; reject duplicate or conflicting active state. Retained historical REVOKED relationships do not by themselves invalidate a different sole valid ACTIVE relationship. Derive the D02 owner from the validated row and compare it to the approved target; the constant is an expected-value check, never a fallback. Refuse absent, revoked, inactive, malformed, detached, stale, ambiguous, wrong-type, cross-tenant or wrong-owner state without disclosing data or causing a store effect. Never cache or reuse authorization across operations.

For list operations, constrain results to the resolved owner, tenant and applicable active status. For a targeted read or mutation, also constrain the exact requested portfolio ID and applicable revision/status; existing restore semantics remain available only for an eligible authorized tombstone. Where an existing child table lacks a tenant column, establish the tenant through its owner/portfolio-scoped parent in the **same** snapshot/transaction rather than pretending the child has a tenant FK. Preserve the existing server-only personal-use switch, manual/broker scope, Gate 4/5 default-deny, broker identity qualification, S1 and Direct P1 behavior. A validated relationship alone grants none of those write permissions. D09 describes the limited case of an in-flight read whose consistent snapshot predates a revocation; it is not permission to reuse a stale context.

**4. Complexity to simplify or defer.** No second principal or tenant, owner per portfolio, owner supplied by a caller, process-global owner cache, general identity federation, or replacement of the generic Direct P1 resolver. No provider label, account name, portfolio name or symbol is authority to choose an owner.

**5. Dependencies.** D01–D03 are approved. D04’s reciprocal conflict guard and D05’s revocation/recovery policy are recommendations only; D06’s ID/audit format is also pending and must not be assumed. D07 must contain all D02 access paths; D09 determines freshness and serialization. D14–D16 affect historical/unknown data coverage; D20 scope and D21/D22 authority boundaries remain unchanged.

**6. Exact recommended decision wording (proposed, not approved):**

> **D08 — APPROVE with clarification (proposed):** For each D02 operation resolve afresh, in the same transaction or read snapshot, the sole ACTIVE existing S1 binding and attached membership and verify the approved S1 principal, binding, membership, tenant and S1 owner values. Require exactly one ACTIVE overall-owner relationship for that membership/type, a matching tenant and an ACTIVE owner-registry entry for `owner-4dc756b3`; reject missing, duplicate, stale, revoked, malformed, detached, conflicting or mismatched state without data or store effect. Do not hide competing active rows by filtering to the expected owner first. Derive the D02 owner from the validated relationship, never from a caller, cache or constant fallback. Scope list results by the resolved owner/tenant and applicable status; scope targeted access by those values plus portfolio ID and applicable revision/status. Keep all four portfolio identities distinct and preserve S1, Direct P1, existing write gates and qualification checks.

**7. Outstanding evidence and consequences.** D04–D06 and D08 remain pending; the exact relationship representation, corrupt/duplicate-state behavior and full read/write call-site inventory have not been approved or tested. There is no evidence here that a relationship or any named portfolio row exists. The current holdings predicate remains fail-closed for the established S1 tuple, and complete historical D02 coverage is unknown pending D14–D16 and applicable authority. Snapshot timing and authorization races require the separate D09 evidence below; no provisioning or D5/D6 action follows.

## D09 — Transaction consistency and concurrency

**1. Existing approved requirements and exact sources.** D01/D03 require fresh valid identity and ACTIVE-state checks; the [local persistence contract §1](../LOCAL-PERSONAL-USE-PERSISTENCE.md#1-what-it-enables) and [§§7.2–7.3](../LOCAL-PERSONAL-USE-PERSISTENCE.md#72-the-authorization-path) preserve in-transaction write checks, audit, revision and idempotency rules. The [operating model’s safeguards](../IPD-OPERATING-MODEL.md#proportionate-safeguards) require data preservation and recoverability. [Package D09](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-DECISION-PACKAGE-2026-11-16.md#d09--transaction-boundaries-nested-work-and-concurrency) and [review D09](IPD-D02-LOCAL-SINGLE-USER-SCOPE-APPLICABILITY-REVIEW-2026-11-15.md#d09--essential-atomic-local-resolution-and-access) propose the relationship-aware transaction contract but do not approve its implementation.

**2. Current single-user applicability.** One user can still issue overlapping IPC requests, import and save concurrently, or encounter a crash. The [store](../../src/persistence/portfolio_store.ts) already contains `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` write handling, read snapshots, revision conflicts and `CommitOutcomeUnknownError`; [the generic resolver](../../src/persistence/binding.ts) opens and commits its own transaction, while [dispatch](../../src/ipc/dispatch.ts) resolves before calling store operations. A store-local read snapshot alone therefore does not demonstrate that a future relationship resolution and its data read share one snapshot. The normal [connection](../../src/persistence/connection.ts) uses local SQLite with foreign keys enabled and DELETE journaling; no concurrency behavior was executed or measured here.

**3. Minimum sufficient design.**

- **Write atomicity and stale state:** On one SQLite connection, begin `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` before resolving the S1/relationship/owner state and authorizing the target; retain that transaction through revision/status checks and all applicable portfolio, holding, contribution and audit effects. Commit once; pre-commit validation, conflict or write failure rolls back the unit. Preserve `expectedRevision`, duplicate/idempotency and existing write-guard semantics. Parsing may occur earlier, but mutable ownership, source-target and revision facts must be rechecked in the transaction.
- **Read consistency:** Start one explicit read transaction on the same connection; establish the snapshot when reading relationship state and use it for the corresponding owner-scoped query and projection. End it with the operation. Do not resolve, commit and then read in another snapshot. One outer transaction wrapper controls the transaction; nested work reuses its context and may not commit independently or open another connection.
- **Revocation and contention:** With a write and revocation serialized as SQLite writers, a revocation committed first is observed and the later write refuses; a write that commits first was ordered before that revocation. A read whose snapshot is established after revocation commits must refuse. An already-started read whose snapshot predates revocation may complete against that earlier consistent view, or block/fail according to actual locking/journal behavior; snapshot consistency is not retroactive cancellation. Do not reuse that read’s context for a new request.
- **Failures and recovery:** A busy/lock or stale-revision refusal must not be converted to success. Do not add automatic retries; any later separately permitted retry must restart the entire operation and re-resolve fresh state. If commit or rollback outcome cannot be established, preserve the existing unknown-outcome/reconciliation behavior; do not claim zero effect or blindly retry. Return no partial read data and, when rollback is confirmed, no partial store effect.

**4. Complexity to simplify or defer.** Reuse local SQLite locking and transaction discipline; no distributed lock, coordinator, queue, cross-host consensus, global cache or multi-user concurrency service. Savepoints are not required for the basic contract; a later nested sub-operation may use one only without independently committing or bypassing outer rollback semantics.

**5. Dependencies.** D01–D03 define identity and tenant checks; D04/D05’s proposed uniqueness and lifecycle, and D06’s proposed lifecycle audit, remain pending. D07 prevents bypass and D08 defines the resolved state. D10–D14 constrain source/provenance checks; D18 concerns SQLite compatibility, and D19 preserves backup/recovery safeguards. No D21/D22 authority follows from transaction design.

**6. Exact recommended decision wording (proposed, not approved):**

> **D09 — APPROVE with clarification (proposed):** For every D02 mutation, use one SQLite `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction and connection from fresh identity/relationship resolution and authorization through revision/status validation and all applicable portfolio, provenance and audit effects; commit once or roll back the complete unit on a confirmed pre-commit failure. For each D02 read, resolve the relationship and query/project the scoped data within one read snapshot and connection. Nested work reuses the outer transaction and cannot commit independently. A revocation committed before a new write transaction or read snapshot is established causes refusal; a read already holding an earlier snapshot may complete consistently against that snapshot or be blocked by SQLite locking. Preserve stale-revision and idempotency rules, report an indeterminate commit/rollback outcome as unknown, and do not blindly retry. No distributed coordination is required for this local scope.

**7. Outstanding evidence and consequences.** No disposable or operational SQLite database was opened and no application/concurrency tests or verifier were run. The bundled runtime’s actual locking, FK, journal and read-snapshot behavior; race ordering; rollback/unknown-commit handling; and integration of every read/write path with one transaction require later authorized synthetic tests. Until those results and the pending design decisions are accepted, do not claim this contract is implemented, instant revocation of in-flight reads, or safe D02 access.

## D10 — Provider representation and catalog

**1. Existing approved requirements and exact sources.** The [approved product determination §1](IPD-D02-OVERALL-PERSONAL-PORTFOLIO-OWNERSHIP-DETERMINATION-2026-11-15.md#1-approved-product-ownership-decision) covers Dhan, Zerodha, other supported brokers and manual holdings under the same D02 owner, while preserving independently manageable portfolios and source/provenance; it does not approve a dynamic provider catalog. The [architecture record §2](IPD-D02-OWNERSHIP-ARCHITECTURE-DECISION-2026-11-15.md#2-product-requirements-confirmed) preserves those distinctions. The local persistence contract [§3](../LOCAL-PERSONAL-USE-PERSISTENCE.md#3-deliberate-projection-scope-contributions-vs-importevidence) retains actual contribution evidence and NULL as unknown; [§4](../LOCAL-PERSONAL-USE-PERSISTENCE.md#4-manual-entries-are-not-broker-observations) preserves the manual-entry distinction. [Package D10](IPD-D02-PROGRAM-AUTHORITY-DECISION-PACKAGE-2026-11-16.md#d10--provider-taxonomy-normalization-and-catalog-governance) and [review D10](IPD-D02-LOCAL-SINGLE-USER-SCOPE-APPLICABILITY-REVIEW-2026-11-15.md#d10--simplify-closed-provider-normalization-not-a-dynamic-catalog) are pending alternatives/recommendations.

**2. Current single-user applicability.** For the four-portfolio example, Dhan and Zerodha need distinct broker origin; Manual A and B share a manual source kind but **not** a portfolio ID or history. [Migration 001](../../src/persistence/migrations/001_initial_schema.ts) records holding `source_broker` and lineage; [migration 004](../../src/persistence/migrations/004_portfolio_contribution_provenance.ts) adds nullable file, broker, digest, import-time and semantics evidence. The [manual mapper](../../src/persistence/manualPersonalPortfolio.ts) uses `manual-personal` and `MANUAL_PERSONAL_ENTRY`. Current [broker import validation](../../src/persistence/brokerImportPersistence.ts) checks that each holding’s source equals the request’s source, but that agreement alone does not prove the selected portfolio’s intended origin. The initial portfolio table has no origin column; the [store](../../src/persistence/portfolio_store.ts) also supports creation of an empty snapshot before source is known. Actual live provenance and target origins are unknown.

The existing [broker type](../../frontend/src/features/portfolio/import/types.ts) and [format detector](../../frontend/src/features/portfolio/import/broker-format-detector.ts) also recognize `GROWW` alongside `DHAN` and `ZERODHA`. This is source evidence of an existing adapter/detection path, **not** a fifth required portfolio or proof of end-to-end D02 support. The product’s “other supported brokers” direction and package D10’s warning against a two-broker hard limit mean the review’s example enum must not be read as a permanently closed two-broker product.

**3. Minimum sufficient design.** Use a small server-controlled, versioned-in-code canonical mapping/allowlist for the currently supported and qualified import paths, not arbitrary caller strings or a dynamically managed provider database. For this example the logical values can be `DHAN`, `ZERODHA` and `MANUAL_PERSONAL`; the existing stored `manual-personal` value maps to the manual meaning without rewriting history. Preserve compatible existing supported provider paths, including the already-recognized Groww adapter where applicable, and extend the mapping when another broker is actually supported under applicable scope. A detector/type alone does not prove that an import is qualified or authorized. Unknown, ambiguous or conflicting source information cannot be treated as a known Dhan/Zerodha/manual origin; do not promote `GENERIC`, `UNKNOWN`, a filename hint or a caller-provided label into proof.

For a new portfolio whose source is already established, bind that origin from qualified broker or manual-path evidence atomically with creation, subject to later applicable decisions and authority. Preserve the existing option to create an empty, unclassified portfolio: where there is no source-bearing history, its first authorized source-specific write may establish and bind the source from qualified evidence in that same transaction, without attributing earlier history. For a source-specific write to a portfolio with source-bearing history, compare a validated canonical source and its row-level evidence with a trustworthy server-owned fact for the selected portfolio’s origin and reject disagreement before effects. If existing evidence cannot establish that fact, keep the historical origin unknown and refuse the source-sensitive mutation until reliable evidence and applicable authority exist; ordinary authorized historical reads need not fabricate an origin. A small durable portfolio-origin field or server-owned mapping **may** be needed if existing evidence is insufficient; that choice is pending D12/D13 and detailed-schema review, not an instruction to add a field. Preserve the distinct portfolio IDs, revisions, broker file/content/lineage/time evidence and manual disposition; never infer origin from portfolio name, symbol, or owner equality. The one-origin-per-example assumption is not an approved general ban on hybrid portfolios.

**4. Complexity to simplify or defer.** Defer a dynamic provider catalog, provider administration/revocation UI, catalog lifecycle, per-account opaque token and root source/link graph unless D11–D13 or a real multi-account/hybrid need justifies them. Preserve old evidence when an adapter is no longer active. Do not impose a permanent two-broker limit, invent a fifth portfolio, change historic source strings, or store credentials/raw account identifiers.

**5. Dependencies.** Approved D01–D03 keep owner/tenant resolution independent of source; D04–D06 remain pending and do not approve source IDs. D07–D09 protect the selected portfolio and transaction. D11 governs any account-level identity/deduplication, D12 source cardinality/hybrid policy, D13 origin/link representation, D14 unknown historical provenance, and D15–D16 historical coverage; all remain pending. D18 and D22 constrain any later schema or operational work.

**6. Exact recommended decision wording (proposed, not approved):**

> **D10 — AMEND (proposed):** Use a small server-controlled canonical source mapping for qualified supported broker adapters and manual entry; the current four-portfolio example distinguishes Dhan, Zerodha and manual sources while preserving separate Manual A and Manual B portfolio IDs and histories. Normalize only evidenced, unambiguous source values and refuse unknown, conflicting or unsupported values for source-specific mutations. When a new portfolio’s source is known, bind its origin from qualified evidence in the creation transaction. Preserve creation of an empty unclassified portfolio; if it has no source-bearing history, its first authorized source-specific write may establish and bind the source atomically from qualified evidence. For an existing portfolio with source history, check its trustworthy server-owned origin and retained evidence before a source-specific write; if its origin cannot be established, refuse rather than guess or backfill it. Retain existing broker/manual provenance and historical unknowns, including the existing manual label, without rewriting them. Preserve other already-supported broker paths and allow later supported providers to be added under the applicable scope; do not turn the example into a permanent two-broker limit. A dynamic provider catalog, account-token scheme and source-link graph are not required by D10 alone; decide any needed portfolio-origin representation and hybrid/account behavior under D11–D14.

**7. Outstanding evidence and consequences.** The repository has not established a trustworthy per-portfolio origin fact for every possible row, nor complete server-side source attestation/alias rules or end-to-end Groww D02 compatibility. Row-to-request source equality is not proof that a write targets the correct portfolio. Actual portfolio rows, source history and historical NULLs were not inspected. D11–D14 must settle account, cardinality, origin/link and unknown-history treatment; if a target’s source cannot be established, source-sensitive writes must remain refused rather than silently merging or inventing provenance. No provider taxonomy, schema, data or implementation is approved by this recommendation.
